**R1 finding 1: closed at design level.** The closure follows from the specified operations and the frozen owners, rather than the proposal's resolution table alone:

1. **Retained evidence and one selected root.** Proposal lines 43–80 extract the root-only portion of `DataReadSession::open_after_missing` into `DataRootHandle`, retaining the absolute selected path, `RepositoryRootEvidence` and capabilities through acquisition. The frozen implementation at `src/storage/repository_state_store.rs:455` already has precisely the lexical normalization, missing-ancestry discovery, capability opening and published-ancestor guard being factored. Commit observation begins afterward at line 468, so this extraction can actually remain root-only. Pending presence and index inspection use the handle reopened against that retained evidence; exec no longer calls `load_index`, whose fresh session at `src/storage/json.rs:842` caused R1's gap.
2. **Revalidation across the handoff and capture.** Proposal steps 3–6 compare retained evidence after bootstrap acquisition, after repository acquisition, after a present-record probe, and after index capture before deciding its result. `CapabilityRoots::open_data` at `src/storage/capability_fs.rs:92` validates the opened directory against the supplied identity through `ensure_capability_identity` at line 491; it does not adopt a replacement's identity. `open_leftover_record` and `inspect_capability_path` operate relative to those capabilities. A persistent replacement therefore cannot become a fresh accepted root under the old guard. Changed/missing evidence fails with conflict; unsafe ancestry/entries and I/O errors retain their typed failures. A root already missing when the lock owner checks its parent retains the explicitly specified `RepositoryNotFoundError`. All these paths return without a child. The final check is a pre-launch admission check, not an atomic exclusion of arbitrary external filesystem renames; the shared lock limitation is assessed separately below.
3. **Predecessor retention is specified.** Proposal lines 85–93 retain the originally acquired bootstrap guard until after repository release. `JsonFileStorage` constructs that predecessor from the data parent at `src/storage/json.rs:515`; raw repository acquisition reenters it, as `src/storage/repo_lock.rs:217` confirms. A changed/missing data identity or symlinked ancestry fails. Moving the same directory inode beneath different safe ancestry can remain admissible: evidence identifies the selected data directory, not its parent. Its internal repository-lock entry moves with it, while the original bootstrap guard remains held and is not reacquired. Exec performs no parent-namespace publication. This matches the existing evidence boundary and does not invent parent identity authority.
4. **Canonical factoring preserves readers.** `DataReadSession` still observes commits on open, checks root evidence on admission and compares commit observations through reopened capabilities. The published-ancestor guard and `discover_data_read_evidence` stay single owners. Its consumers still receive `Moved`/busy admission and bounded retry behavior without writer locks or replay. Mechanical field-access migration also includes `ConfigurationObservation::admit` at line 618, in the same authorized file. Commands receive `DataRootHold`; they neither compare identities nor build a layout. D-20, dependency direction, semantic types, convergence and canonical cutover are satisfied by this factoring.

Walking R1's counterexample independently: precheck retains evidence for A at path P; bootstrap is acquired; repository acquisition opens A's lock and waits; A is renamed and initialized B is published at P; A's lock becomes available.
